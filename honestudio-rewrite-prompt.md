## Repositioning Brief

**Old positioning:** "Fractional Ops & Tech Partner — Web / AI / Strategy, three equal pillars"

**New positioning:** Lead with software development and AI-tool building. Strategy and ops capabilities exist to make the software fit the business — they are supporting context, not co-equal services.

**Why this change:**
- "Fractional Ops" is an established category owned by part-time COOs doing SOPs and accountability systems — not what Niell does. It signals the wrong buyer.
- Three co-equal pillars read as generalist. Specialist positioning commands higher rates and attracts more qualified inbound.
- The strongest proof (VC AI platform) is a software build. The positioning should match the best work.
- The "operator who codes" angle is genuinely rare and worth centering — not burying in a three-column grid.

**New headline direction:** Something that establishes Niell as a developer who thinks like a business operator. He does not just build what he is briefed — he figures out what needs to be built, then builds it.

**Target buyers (in priority order):**
1. Founders and lean teams at startups who need custom internal tools, AI workflows, or a web app — and do not have a technical co-founder or internal dev team
2. Small businesses and e-commerce operators whose existing tools are not talking to each other and need someone to clean up the stack and automate the repetitive work
3. Agencies and consultants who need a reliable white-label technical execution partner

**Tone:** Direct, specific, confident without being arrogant. Conversational but smart. The About page already gets this right — match that register everywhere. No corporate hedging. No generic agency language ("bespoke solutions," "full-service," "end-to-end"). No em dashes. No emojis.

## Copy Rules (Non-Negotiable)

These apply across every page and every piece of copy produced:

1. **No em dashes.** Use a comma, a period, or a colon instead. If a sentence feels like it needs an em dash, rewrite the sentence.

2. **No emojis.** Anywhere on the site.

3. **No placeholder zeros.** If a stat cannot be confirmed with a real number, use a bracketed placeholder: [X years], [X projects]. Never output "0+" or "0%" as visible site copy.

4. **No "solutions," "bespoke," "end-to-end," "full-service," "leverage," "robust," "streamline," "innovate," "cutting-edge," "game-changer," "empower," "holistic," "synergy," "unlock," "elevate," "harness," "supercharge."** These are generic and AI-sounding. If you reach for one of these words, find the specific thing it is trying to describe and name that instead.

5. **No AI sentence patterns.** Specifically: do not write "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." Do not write self-answered rhetorical questions ("The result? A mess."). Do not write negative parallelism ("It's not X, it's Y") as the primary structure of a section. Do not stack tricolons (rule of three) back-to-back. Do not use "-ing phrase" tails to add shallow analysis ("highlighting its importance," "reflecting broader trends").

6. **Lead with the reader's pain, not the product's features.** Every service section should start with what the prospect is experiencing, not what Niell offers.

7. **One primary CTA per page.** Supporting CTAs are fine, but one action should dominate each page. That action is almost always "Book a Call" or "Start a Conversation."

8. **Specificity over scale.** "13,000+ hours of manual research eliminated" is better than "saved the team significant time." Use the real number every time you have it.

9. **First person throughout.** Niell is the author. "I build," "I handle," "I'd rather" — not "Hone Studio delivers" or "our team provides."

10. **Hone Studio public-facing rule:** Do not name Showcase Ventures, Spotlight, or Spyglass by name in any public-facing copy UNLESS the case study has been explicitly approved for named attribution. If uncertain, use "a media and consumer VC firm" for the company and describe the tools functionally rather than by name.
